Emerging framing that applies quantum computing (often with AI/ML) to digital forensic analysis, recovery, cryptographic work, and long-term evidence-security planning.

## Definition
Quantum forensics is the proposed use of quantum computing principles—such as superposition, entanglement, and quantum parallelism—together with AI techniques to process large or cryptographically hard forensic problems beyond classical capacity.

## Claims commonly made in source literature
- Faster search and reconstruction over large, fragmented, or multi-location datasets.
- Accelerated cryptographic analysis relative to classical methods (including algorithms such as Shor’s and Grover’s in future-threat narratives).
- Quantum machine learning (QML) as a route to high-dimensional pattern recognition in logs, media, or obfuscated artefacts.
- Parallel need for **quantum-resistant (post-quantum) cryptography** to protect forensic data and tooling as cryptanalytic capability grows.

## Maturity and caution
- Practical, scalable quantum hardware and error-corrected forensic workflows remain immature; treat operational claims as research/roadmap material, not validated methods.
- Dual use is explicit: the same cryptanalytic power that might unlock evidence also threatens current encryption and privacy expectations.
- Any justice-facing deployment would still require method validation, uncertainty disclosure, bias/audit controls, and lawful-authority constraints—quantum speed does not replace those duties.
