A taxonomy-style ontology that partitions digital forensics into nested disciplines and subdisciplines to support shared vocabulary, education, certification, and tool scoping.

## Definition
A digital forensic disciplines ontology organises the field into hierarchical categories—typically disciplines, subdisciplines, objects, and finer subobjects—so communities can describe specialisations consistently and extend the model as technologies change.

## Canonical top-level disciplines (Karie & Venter, 2014)
- Computer forensics
- Software forensics
- Database forensics
- Multimedia forensics
- Device forensics
- Network forensics

## What it is for
- Curriculum and module design (including prerequisite planning)
- Professional specialisation and certification scoping
- Tool coverage mapping across DF subareas
- Shared high-level semantics for talking about “what kind of DF work” is in scope

## What it is not
- Not a substitute for investigation-evidence exchange models such as [[uco-a-unified-cybersecurity-ontology]] / CASE / DFAX
- Not by itself a machine-reasoning layer for artefact validation (contrast AGAFA’s OWL + ASP use)
- Not an exhaustive inventory of every device or artefact type

## Relationship to AGAFA and later DF ontology work
- Helps bound AGAFA **domain coverage claims** (which disciplines a pilot supports) without defining case graph schemas.
- Complements later SLR and UCO/CASE literature: use disciplines ontology for **field partitioning**; use UCO/CASE for **interoperable case representation**.
- Aligns loosely with CyBOK-style competency framing for teaching pathways ([[cyber-security-body-of-knowledge-cybok]]).
