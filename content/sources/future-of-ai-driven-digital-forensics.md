Chapter-only ingest of Iyengar et al. (2025) Ch. 12, framing near- and longer-term AI roles in digital forensics around efficiency/accuracy/scalability, redundancy reduction, AI-assisted recovery, tool security, and speculative AI–quantum synergy.

## Citation
Iyengar, S. S., Nabavirazavi, S., Hariprasad, Y., Prasad, H. B., & Mohan, C. K. (2025). Future of AI-driven digital forensics. In *Artificial Intelligence in Practice: Theory and Application for Cyber Security and Forensics* (pp. 335–362). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-89327-8_12

## Scope note
- Only **Chapter 12** of the parent book was ingested for this wiki; other chapters remain uningested.
- Raw file `Future of AI Driven Digital Forensics.pdf` is the full book; the source page title tracks the chapter (and filename), not the book title.
- Tone is agenda-setting and forward-looking. Several enterprise/law-enforcement “case studies” read as illustrative scenarios rather than independently evidenced primary case reports.
- Quantum-forensics and quantum-decryption claims are speculative relative to current operational DF capability and to this wiki’s UK method-validation / ACPO baseline.

## Key points
- Positions AI as addressing DF volume/complexity via **efficiency**, **accuracy**, and **scalability**, while listing emerging computational challenges: data density, data quality, privacy/security/compliance, real-time analysis, **result interpretability / AI explainability**, and **model drift**.
- Argues redundancy (duplicate/near-duplicate artefacts across devices and stores) inflates cost/time and can bury unique evidence; proposes AI techniques including deduplication/pruning, pattern recognition, historical ML filtering, and clustering/similarity detection.
- Frames AI-assisted data recovery for encrypted, corrupted, fragmented, cloud/RAID, and obfuscated stores using deep learning, predictive modelling, and pattern recognition — including contested suggestions that models might assist decryption when keys are unavailable.
- Treats security of AI-driven forensic tooling as first-class: adversarial model tampering, training/ops data breach risk, cloud transit/at-rest exposure, and misuse (including fabricated evidence); also describes AI for anomaly/tamper detection, adaptive encryption/access control, and predictive vulnerability monitoring.
- Introduces **quantum forensics** as an emerging AI–quantum synergy theme (accelerated crypto analysis, large-scale search/reconstruction, TensorFlow Quantum as an example QML stack) alongside **quantum-resistant / post-quantum** security needs as quantum cryptanalysis matures.
- Predicts stronger automation, multi-modal correlation, behavioural analytics, and a shift toward more **proactive / predictive** forensics; flags that legal systems will need clearer standards for validating AI algorithms, explaining decisions, managing bias, and balancing quantum-era decryption capability against privacy rights.
